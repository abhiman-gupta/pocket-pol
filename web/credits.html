Pocket Pol is a browser renderer for polarized light, written in JavaScript and WebGPU. It brings the polarization models of Pollia, a research renderer built on Mitsuba 3, to the web. Pocket Pol stands on the work of the people and projects below.

Mitsuba 3

Mitsuba 3 is a research-oriented renderer for forward and inverse light transport, developed at EPFL’s Realistic Graphics Lab. Wenzel Jakob created the project. Its preferred software citation names Wenzel Jakob, Sébastien Speierer, Nicolas Roussel, Merlin Nimier-David, Delio Vicini, Tizian Zeltner, Baptiste Nicolet, Miguel Crespo, Vincent Leroy, and Ziyi Zhang. Thank you to everyone who has contributed to Mitsuba.

Pollia is a fork of Mitsuba 3, so the polarization rendering that Pocket Pol adapts rests on Mitsuba’s design. Pocket Pol’s Cornell box geometry also follows Mitsuba’s built-in Cornell box scene.

Pollia

Pollia is a research renderer for polarized light transport, custom polarimetric BRDFs, and Stokes image generation. It is built as a fork of Mitsuba 3 and developed by Charlie Tribble, Abhiman Gupta, and Matthew Kupinski.

Pocket Pol adapts these parts of Pollia:

  • the layout of its Swift interactive window
  • its implementation of the Jarecki material model, with RGB and monochrome channels
  • its polarized area emitter and Mueller-matrix path transport
  • its polarization display filters and color maps

Pollia is the reference for checking Pocket Pol’s output.

Pollia source · Revision a8c7d7ea, used by Pocket Pol

PolRay

Several pieces come from PolRay, a polarization ray tracer:

  • Pocket Pol’s default Cornell box: its layout, camera framing, and RGB diffuse reflectances
  • Pollia’s nylon material parameters and display color tables

Jarecki material model

Pocket Pol’s material model follows the depolarizing Mueller BRDF developed by Quinn Jarecki and Meredith Kupinski.

Quinn Jarecki and Meredith Kupinski. “Efficient Modeling of Depolarizing Mueller BRDFs.” arXiv:2307.11976, 2023. doi:10.48550/arXiv.2307.11976
